Editing two pages in fifty can produce another full copy instead of a small continued revision. The author wants to tell a story freely, preserve the reasoning and original evidence, and return to the same working document for the next conversation.

This is an account of why the project exists, how its design addresses those needs, how a person uses it, and where its ideas and code came from. The introduction must retain motives, architecture, a reasoned demonstration, practical teaching and formal acknowledgments.

## Visual design and actual Section pages

Evidence decides the composition. Most storytelling and explanation pages should have a meaningful image, screenshot, figure, video or diagram as their principal content. Text gives that visual a purpose, reading order and interpretation. Informational pages remain useful when the information actually needs that form.

Ordinary content pages need purposeful density. Compact headings and readable explanations should leave room for substantial evidence. Empty space, huge body text, a very small screenshot and a restrained palette do not establish quality by themselves.

**Title-only Section pages are required for these introduction projects.** They are real slides at narrative turns, containing only the title: no subtitle, watermark, number, logo or decoration. Maintain Section metadata separately. A `pageType: section` field on a content-filled page does not meet this requirement.

Do not use decorative middle-dot separators, text-arrow chains, thin line arrows, generic accent bars or a repeated card grid as the default visual language. Use purposeful graphical relationships where needed. Scientific symbols, plot labels and genuine source content retain their meaning. These are this project's explicit design instructions, not a universal ban imposed on all HUANDENG users.

A UI explanation must convey location and detail together. Establish the actual whole Editor and identify where the demonstrated area sits. A legible enlarged detail can use a restrained region outline and matched location marker. A crop alone is insufficient, and adding an unreadable whole-window thumbnail to every page is not a substitute for visual organization.

Comparisons should align the objects being compared, use a consistent reference frame and make changed and retained regions understandable. A connecting arrow alone does not explain the relationship. Real page previews and screenshots are evidence; conceptual diagrams must be recognizable as diagrams.

There is no numerical “human feeling” or “AI feeling” score. Review real pages at presentation size and read their sequence. Technical checks can detect faults; they cannot certify taste.

## Story, Notes and editable objects

Use the existing Story model. Project/Main Story explains the overall purpose; Section Story explains a meaningful grouping; page Story records the page's contribution, requested change and preservation constraints; Presentation Story adapts the work to a particular talk. Notes contain the spoken explanation. Do not add a parallel outline database.

Develop Notes with the pages. They should let a speaker explain the visual and transition to what follows. They are not a copy of labels or a marketing script added after the design. Internal production details belong in supporting records or Notes unless the audience needs them to understand the claim.

Use native tables for genuine row/column relationships. Do not imitate cells with many text boxes, shapes or rules. Ordinary prose and lists should use one rich-text object where practical. Scientific panels, evidence labels and diagram nodes can remain separate when their geometry carries meaning.

Preserve original cited figure pixels where the source itself is evidence. Do not replace a source figure with a generated imitation or use a flattened fake table to evade editable-object requirements.

## Audit, Spec, Implement, Validate, Release

1. **Audit.** Read the complete author material, applicable policies, previous versions and actual renders. Identify preserved work, missing narrative, design failures, material provenance and verified product behavior. Distinguish source words from agent interpretation.
2. **Spec.** Organize the argument, place teaching where it resolves a narrative need, and choose the actual evidence and visual relationships. Frame representative pages within this stage: the founding problem, architecture, a located Editor detail, a comparison/scientific case, credits and a real Section pause.
3. **Implement.** Work through the shared writer using current identities and revisions. Keep source material, meaningful history and stable page identities. Build independently sourced visuals and professional plots, then place them deliberately.
4. **Validate.** Inspect every final page and the whole sequence in the actual Editor and exported rendering. Review narrative, UI orientation, visual hierarchy, plot meaning, real title-only pauses, complete credits, readable typography, source independence and accurate Notes. Revise a weak result even when its operations and schema pass.
5. **Release.** Save the reviewable project and required exports, preserve source attribution, and describe what was actually verified. Follow the repository's version/release policy for product changes. An agent review or successful native process is not the author's acceptance.

Frame is part of Spec, not an extra stage that bypasses the author's requested order. Routine authorized edits do not require an invented confirmation gate. A changed design may require revisiting its argument; document the reason instead of silently changing the objective.
